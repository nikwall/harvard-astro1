Definitions
Star · planet · moon
A star is a large glowing ball of gas powered by nuclear fusion. A planet is a moderately large object orbiting a star that has cleared its orbit (Pluto → “dwarf planet”, 2006). A moon (satellite) orbits a planet.
Asteroid · comet
Small rocky (asteroid) or icy (comet) bodies orbiting a star.
Nebula · galaxy
A nebula is an interstellar cloud of gas and dust. A galaxy is an island of stars, gas and dust (millions to trillions of stars) held together by gravity.
Astronomical unit (AU)
Earth's average distance from the Sun, ≈ 150 million km.
Light-year (ly)
The distance light travels in one year, ≈ 9.46 trillion km (≈ 1013 km).
Rotation · orbit
Rotation is spin about an axis; orbit (revolution) is motion around another object.

Scale models make the numbers human. On the book's “Voyage” scale (1 to 10 billion), the Sun is a large grapefruit (14 cm), Earth is a ballpoint (1 mm) 15 m away, Jupiter a marble 78 m away. The nearest star system (Alpha Centauri) would be another grapefruit 4400 km away, roughly across the USA. Space is overwhelmingly empty.

Counting stars: counting the Milky Way's 100+ billion stars at one per second would take 3000+ years. The observable universe holds ~1022 stars, comparable to the grains of dry sand on all of Earth's beaches.

Looking back in time: light takes time to travel, so the farther we look, the further back in time we see. We see Andromeda as it was 2.5 million years ago. The observable universe is the part from which light has had time to reach us in the ~14 billion years since the Big Bang. It is not necessarily the whole universe.

Toolkit · distance = speed × time
Light-year$$1\,\text{ly} = c \times 1\,\text{yr} = \left(3.0\times10^{5}\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{s}}\right)\left(3.15\times10^{7}\,\text{s}\right) \approx 9.5\times10^{12}\,\text{km}$$1 yr = 365 × 24 × 60 × 60 s ≈ π × 107 s, a handy memory trick.
Light-travel (lookback) time$$t = \frac{d}{c}\qquad \text{object at } N \text{ ly} \Rightarrow \text{we see it as it was } N \text{ years ago}$$Sunlight: t = 1.5×108 km ÷ 3×105 km/s = 500 s ≈ 8.3 min.
Scale models: scale factor = model size ÷ real size. On a 1 : 1010 scale, the Earth–Sun distance becomes 1.5×1011 m ÷ 1010 = 15 m, and Alpha Centauri (4.1×1016 m) becomes 4.1×106 m = 4100 km.

1.2 The history of the universe: how did we come to be?

~14 Gyrage of the universe (book); 13.8 Gyr measured
4.5 Gyrage of the solar system ≈ ⅓ of cosmic age
>1011stars in the Milky Way
~1011galaxies in the observable universe
  • Big Bang, ~14 billion years ago (best value today: 13.8 Gyr). The universe has been expanding ever since, except where gravity pulled matter together into galaxies.
  • The Big Bang made almost only hydrogen and helium. Everything heavier (the carbon in your cells, the oxygen you breathe, the iron in your blood) was forged by fusion inside stars and scattered by stellar deaths.
  • Galactic recycling: stars form from gas → forge heavier elements → return enriched gas to space → new stars and planets form from it. Our solar system formed from such recycled material ~4.5 billion years ago. We are “star stuff”.
  • We are latecomers. On the cosmic calendar (the whole history squeezed into one year), the Sun forms in early September, dinosaurs die out on December 30, and all of recorded history fits into the last ~11 seconds before midnight on December 31.

1.3 Spaceship Earth: how are we moving through space?

Key insight · the raisin cake
Bake a raisin cake that doubles in size: raisins 1, 2, 3 cm from “our” raisin end up 2, 4, 6 cm away, so they recede at 1, 2, 3 cm/h. Farther ⇒ faster, and every raisin sees the same pattern. So the fact that all distant galaxies recede does not put us at the center. Run the movie backward and everything was together: the Big Bang. The expansion rate gives the age (age ≈ 1/expansion rate). Dark matter (unseen mass, most of the galaxy's mass) and dark energy (drives accelerating expansion) are introduced here and return in Ch. 15–18.

Myth vs. reality
✗ “It'll take me light-years to finish.” ✓ A light-year is a distance, not a time.
✗ Solar system ≈ galaxy. ✓ The galaxy is ~108 times wider and holds >1011 star systems.
✗ All galaxies recede ⇒ we're at the center. ✓ Every observer in a uniformly expanding universe sees the same thing.
✗ “Expanding universe” means everything grows. ✓ Only the spaces between galaxy groups grow; gravity holds you, Earth and galaxies together.

Practice Exam-style questions with worked answers

Quick QuizCorrect cosmic address from small to large?
Earth → solar system → Milky Way → Local Group → Local Supercluster → universe. (The Local Group is a group of galaxies, so the Milky Way comes before it.)
Quick QuizBetelgeuse is ~600 ly away. If it explodes tonight, when do we find out?
Not for ~600 years: the news travels at the speed of light. (Conversely, if we saw it explode tonight, it actually happened ~600 years ago.)
Quick QuizOn a scale where you walk from the Sun to Pluto in a few minutes, how big are planets and how far are the stars?
Planets are marble-size or smaller; the nearest stars are thousands of miles away. Scale: Sun–Pluto ≈ 6×109 km → a few hundred m ⇒ factor ~1010.
Quick QuizCould we see a galaxy 50 billion light-years away?
No. Its light would need 50 Gyr to reach us, longer than the universe's age, so it lies beyond our observable universe (in the book's light-travel picture). A bigger telescope doesn't help.
Quick QuizIf stars existed but galaxies did not, would we exist?
Probably not: the heavy elements in us were made by earlier stars and recycled within a galaxy into the cloud that formed the Sun. Without galaxies there's no recycling.
ProblemAllen measures an expansion rate 50% faster than Wendy's. Whose universe is older, and by how much?
Age ∝ 1/(expansion rate). Faster expansion ⇒ less time to reach today's separations ⇒ Allen's age is younger: 1/1.5 = ⅔ of Wendy's (e.g., 9.3 vs. 14 Gyr).
ProblemHow fast must you travel to reach Alpha Centauri (4.4 ly) in 100 years? Compare with spacecraft (~50,000 km/h).
v = 4.4 ly / 100 yr = 0.044 c = 0.044 × 1.08×109 km/h ≈ 4.8×107 km/h, about 950× (≈103×) faster than our fastest spacecraft.
Does it make sense?“Photos of distant galaxies show them as they were when they were much younger.”
Makes sense. We see light that left them long ago (lookback time), so deep images are baby pictures of galaxies. That's how astronomers study galaxy evolution (Ch. 16).
ProblemHow long does light take from the Moon (384,000 km) and from the Sun (1.5×108 km)?
t = d/c: Moon 3.84×105 ÷ 3.0×105 ≈ 1.3 s; Sun 1.5×108 ÷ 3.0×105 = 500 s ≈ 8.3 min. You always see the Sun as it was 8 minutes ago.

Hirshfeld Activity 22 · Earth's history as one calendar year (a likely homework format)
Event (years ago)Calendar date
Earth forms (4.6 Gyr)Jan 1, 0:00

Oxygen builds up (2.4 Gyr)Jun 24

A 20-year lifelast 0.14 s
Method: squeeze Earth's 4.6 billion years into 365 days. An event N years ago falls T = (N / 4.6×109) × 365 days before midnight on Dec 31. For small T, convert to hours (×24), minutes (×1440) or seconds (×86,400).
Worked: dinosaurs, N = 6.6×107 yr: T = 0.0143 × 365 = 5.2 days before year's end → Dec 26, ~6 pm. Humans: T = 0.024 d = 34 min → 11:26 pm on Dec 31.
What to take away: life is old (most of the year), complex life is recent (the last six weeks), and all of history fits in the final minute. The book's cosmic calendar (earlier in this chapter) does the same with the 14-billion-year universe; Pasachoff & Filippenko use a 24-hour day, on which Earth forms 8 hours ago and humans appear 2 seconds ago.
Going deeper · the universe's “phone number” (Owocki §1.2)
Count powers of ten from atomic nucleus to the edge of the observable universe as a phone number, 555-711-2555. Area code: nucleus (10−15 m) → atom (10−10) → cell (10−5) → you (1 m), steps of 5. Then 7 to Earth (107 m), 1 to Jupiter, 1 to the Sun, 2 to the AU (1011 m); then 5 to the nearest star (1016 m), 5 to the Galaxy (1021 m), 5 to the observable universe (1026 m). Speeds span 9 powers of ten from walking to light. And 1 yr ≈ π×107 s, a handy shortcut.
OwockiHow fast does the equator move with Earth's rotation? Earth in its orbit, as a fraction of c?
2π × 6378 km / 24 h ≈ 1670 km/h (465 m/s). Orbit: 2π AU/yr = 30 km/s = 10−4 c.
CosmosScale model: 1 AU = 15 km. Where is Proxima Centauri (4.0×1013 km)?
4.0×1013/1.5×108 × 15 km = 4×106 km: ten times the real Earth–Moon distance, even in the shrunk model.
OwockiThe Sun is ~25,000 ly from the galactic center and orbits in 200 Myr. Speed?
2π × 25,000 ly / 2×108 yr ≈ 8×10−4 ly/yr ≈ 230 km/s.

30-second
recap
Earth is one planet around an ordinary star, in one galaxy among ~1011. Distances are so large that we measure them with light-travel time, which also means looking far = looking back in time. The universe began ~14 Gyr ago and is expanding (farther galaxies recede faster; no center). Its H and He were turned into heavier elements by stars and recycled by galaxies into us: we are star stuff.
