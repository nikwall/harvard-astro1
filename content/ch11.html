11.1 Why does the Sun shine, and what's inside?

Not chemical burning (that would last only a few thousand years) and not contraction alone (tens of millions of years). The Sun began shining ~4.6 Gyr ago when gravitational contraction heated its core enough for nuclear fusion, and fusion has powered it ever since. It stays steady thanks to two balances:

  1. Gravitational (hydrostatic) equilibrium: at every depth the outward push of pressure balances the inward pull of gravity (the weight of the layers above). Deeper means more weight, so pressure, density and temperature all rise inward.
  2. Energy balance: energy released by fusion in the core = energy radiated from the surface.

Layers: the core (fusion), the radiation zone (energy carried by photons), the convection zone (rising hot gas and sinking cool gas, visible as granulation on the surface), and the photosphere, the “surface” where light escapes. Above it lie the chromosphere (~104 K), the corona (1–2 million K, seen in X rays and during eclipses) and the solar wind, a stream of charged particles flowing out through the solar system.

11.2 Nuclear fusion in the Sun

Protons repel each other electrically. Only at very high temperature and density do they collide fast enough to get within range of the short-range strong force, which binds them. The Sun's core (15 MK, 150× water's density) fuses hydrogen by the proton–proton chain:

Step 1¹H + ¹H → ²H (deuterium) + positron + neutrino (happens twice)
Step 2²H + ¹H → ³He + gamma ray (happens twice)
Step 3³He + ³He → ⁴He + ¹H + ¹H
Net result4 ¹H → ⁴He + 2 e⁺ + 2 ν + energy. The He is 0.7% lighter than the four H: E = mc².

The solar thermostat: fusion is extremely temperature-sensitive. If the core heats up, fusion rises, the extra pressure makes the core expand and cool, and fusion drops back. If the core cools, it contracts and heats. So the two balances keep the fusion rate steady.

Getting the energy out: a photon made in the core is absorbed and re-emitted in random directions countless times: a random walk through the radiation zone that takes ~100,000+ years. Then convection carries the energy the last 30% of the radius in weeks. Checking the model: we can't see inside, so we build mathematical models from physical laws and test them against the Sun's size, temperature and luminosity, plus (1) helioseismology (vibrations of the surface reveal the interior like seismic waves) and (2) solar neutrinos, which fly straight out from the core. Early experiments caught only ⅓ of the predicted neutrinos (the “solar neutrino problem”). The answer, confirmed in 2001–02, was that neutrinos change type on the way (oscillations): the model was right.

11.3 The Sun–Earth connection

Solar activity comes from magnetic fields. The Sun rotates faster at the equator (~25 days) than at the poles (~30 days); this differential rotation plus convection twists field lines until they poke through the surface:

  • Sunspots: regions of strong magnetic field where hot plasma can't flow in. They're cooler (~4000 K), so they look dark by comparison but would glow brightly on their own.
  • Prominences (loops of gas along field lines), flares (snapping field lines releasing energy in minutes) and coronal mass ejections (billions of tons of plasma hurled outward). Magnetic energy also heats the chromosphere and corona.
  • Effects on Earth: charged particles hit the magnetosphere and trigger auroras and geomagnetic storms that endanger satellites, astronauts and power grids. The May 2024 storm brought auroras as far south as Florida and Mexico.
Myth vs. reality
✗ The Sun is on fire. ✓ No chemistry or oxygen is involved. It glows like hot embers (thermal radiation), and the heat is resupplied by nuclear fusion in the core.
✗ Sunspots are holes or black. ✓ They're magnetically cooled regions (4000 K), dark only by contrast.

Practice Exam-style questions with worked answers

Quick QuizGreatest mass: one He nucleus, four electrons, or four protons?
Four protons: the helium nucleus is ~0.7% lighter. That missing mass is the energy fusion releases.
Quick QuizCoolest layer: core, radiation zone or photosphere? X-ray images show…?
The photosphere (5800 K) is coolest. X-ray images show the million-degree corona.
Quick QuizHow do we know the core temperature? What proves fusion is happening?
Mathematical models built on physical laws. Solar neutrinos give direct evidence of core fusion.
Quick QuizEnergy leaves the core as…? What drives the activity cycle?
Photons (radiation). The cycle comes from changes in the organization of the Sun's magnetic field, not in its fusion rate.
Quick QuizGreatest hazard to satellites?
Charged particles from flares and CMEs: they damage electronics and heat the upper atmosphere, increasing drag.
ProblemWien peaks for the Sun (5800 K) and a sunspot (4000 K)?
2.9×106/5800 = 500 nm (blue-green; the Sun looks white because it emits all colors strongly). Sunspot: 725 nm (red) and (4000/5800)4 ≈ 23% as bright per m².
ProblemMass the Sun loses to fusion in 10 Gyr (constant L)?
E = 3.8×1026 W × 3.15×1017 s = 1.2×1044 J, so m = E/c² ≈ 1.3×1027 kg ≈ 220 Earth masses (only 0.07% of the Sun).
ProblemPhotosphere pressures: top (1.6×1016 cm−3, 4500 K) vs. bottom (1.5×1017, 7000 K)?
P ∝ nT: bottom/top = (1.5×1017 × 7000)/(1.6×1016 × 4500) ≈ 15×. Deeper layers support more overlying weight (gravitational equilibrium).
ProblemSun: 2×1030 kg, 75% H, 13% of that usable, fusing 6×1011 kg/s. Lifetime?
Fuel = 2×1030 × 0.75 × 0.13 ≈ 2×1029 kg, so t = 3.3×1017 s ≈ 1010 yr. It's ~4.6 Gyr in, so ~5–6 Gyr are left.

Going deeper · three timescales and a lucky coincidence (Owocki §8, 15–18)
Chemical (ε ~ 10−9 of mc²): ~104 yr. Kelvin–Helmholtz (shrinking): t ≈ GM²/(RL) ≈ 30 Myr, too short for geology. Nuclear (0.007 × 0.1 × Mc²/L): 10 Gyr. 30-second
recap
The Sun is a ball of H/He plasma held steady by gravitational equilibrium (pressure vs. gravity) and energy balance (fusion in = light out), with fusion's temperature sensitivity acting as a thermostat. The proton–proton chain turns 4 H into He, and 0.7% of the mass becomes energy (E = mc²). Energy random-walks out as photons, then convects, then escapes the photosphere. Neutrinos and helioseismology confirm the models. Magnetic fields make sunspots, flares and CMEs on an 11-year cycle, and space weather reaches Earth.
